# NASA: Beyond Earth (V4) — sources and credits

The presentation combines NASA and partner imagery with Blender animations using NASA models and data and original project elements. Credits are retained in the speaker notes and hidden slide 15 (REFERENCES). In V4.1, most ANIMATION production labels were removed. Removing a label does not change the provenance of the underlying material.

## Facts used in the notes (checked on nasa.gov / science.nasa.gov)

- NASA began operations 1 October 1958; Sputnik launched 4 October 1957.
- Alan Shepard, Freedom 7 (Mercury-Redstone 3), 5 May 1961, ~15-minute suborbital flight; Kennedy's Moon speech 25 May 1961.
- Saturn V: 110.6 m tall, ~2,800 t fuelled, five F-1 engines, ~34.5 MN (7.5 million lbf) thrust; 13 launches 1967–1973.
- Apollo 8: first crew to orbit the Moon, Earthrise photo by Bill Anders, 24 December 1968; orbit ~110 km (60 nautical miles).
- Apollo 11: landing 20 July 1969, Armstrong and Aldrin in Eagle, Collins in Columbia; 12 Moonwalkers 1969–1972. At Tranquility Base Earth stood about 65° above the horizon. Lunar dust: with no air, grains follow ballistic (parabolic) paths and land; they do not billow or hang. At engine cut-off no new dust is lifted, but grains already in flight keep going until they land.
- ISS: continuously crewed since 2 November 2000; ~400 km, ~28,000 km/h, ~16 orbits a day; about the size of a football field.
- Hubble: launched 24 April 1990 on Discovery (STS-31), 2.4 m mirror. It observes ultraviolet, visible and near-infrared light (WFC3/IR up to about 1.7 micrometres), so it cannot see the most strongly redshifted early galaxies; the Pillars image on slide 7 is Hubble's near-infrared view. Webb covers about 0.6 to 28 micrometres.
- Webb: launched 25 December 2021; 6.5 m mirror, 18 segments; the two mirror wings deployed 7 and 8 January 2022; works near L2, 1.5 million km away; sunshield about the size of a tennis court.
- Perseverance: landed in Jezero Crater 18 February 2021; top speed ~4.2 cm/s; passed 45.22 km on 11 October 2026, beating Opportunity's off-world driving record (NASA Perseverance blog, 12 October 2026).
- Cheyava Falls: sampled July 2024; NASA (10 September 2025) called it the clearest potential biosignature found on Mars so far.
- SLS: 98 m, ~39 MN (8.8 million lbf), about 15% more thrust than Saturn V.
- Artemis II: launched 1 April 2026 (Wiseman, Glover, Koch, Hansen); on 6 April 2026 reached 406,771 km from Earth, beating Apollo 13's 400,171 km record; splashdown 10 April 2026.
- Artemis III: planned for 2027 as a crewed test in Earth orbit of rendezvous and docking with commercial lunar landers; first landing planned for Artemis IV in 2028.

## Sound

Only slide 3 has sound (the Saturn V film's own track, normalised to −24 LUFS, volume 45% in PowerPoint). All space scenes are silent on purpose: there is no sound in a vacuum. No music or third-party audio is used, and the deck works fully muted.
